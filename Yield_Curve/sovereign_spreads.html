02

How do you measure the price of risk?

Two prices: the spread over Germany and the CDS premium.

Why Germany?
Germany is the euro area's "safe" borrower. Every other euro country borrows in the same currency, so the gap between its 10-year yield and Germany's is a clean measure of how risky the market thinks it is.
Spread
(10-year yield of the country − 10-year yield of Germany) × 100, in basis points.
Basis point (bp)
0.01 percentage point; a 150 bp spread = 1.50% more per year.
Credit default swap (CDS)
An insurance contract on a government's debt. The buyer pays a yearly premium (quoted in bp of the amount insured) and gets paid if the country defaults.
Recovery rate
The share of the money you would get back after a default; 40% is the market convention.
Implied default probability
Yearly default probability ≈ CDS premium ÷ (1 − recovery rate): the default risk the market is implying.

Why now, and what happened in October?

France has a budget and government problem; Italy has more debt but a stable government.

France

  • Problem: deficit 5.4% of GDP (EU limit 3%), debt 119% of GDP, a minority government (PM Lecornu) that must pass the 2027 budget, and an election in May 2027.
  • Protests: student protests since September, then 550,000+ marched against the cost of living on 17 Oct. Looting, Limoges town hall burned, 1,200 arrests.
  • Market: spread back above 150 bp, the 2011 record; 10y yield near 5%.

Italy

  • Problem: even more debt (~135% of GDP), but a stable government since 2022, a falling deficit and rating upgrades in 2025. The diesel price shock hurts.
  • Protests: truckers and farmers block roads in the South over diesel (16 Oct, fuel shortages); riots in Milan, Turin and Bologna on 17-18 Oct, 141 officers injured.
  • Market: spread jumped to 131 bp on 5 Oct, then eased to ~107 bp.

06

What did we learn?

Five takeaways.

  1. France now pays as much as Italy, or more. France paid more than Italy in 5 of the last 12 months (Dec 2025, Jan 2026, Feb 2026, Aug 2026, Sep 2026).
  2. France is back at its euro-crisis level: 154 bp on 20 Oct, against a monthly record of 154 bp in Nov 2011. Italy, at 107 bp, is far from its own record of 519 bp (Nov 2011).
  3. Default insurance tells the same story. France's 5-year CDS is 79.5 bp (~1.33% default risk per year), Italy's 68.0 bp (~1.13%): 10.9x and 9.3x Germany's premium.
  4. The reasons differ. France: a large deficit and a minority government that must pass the 2027 budget. Italy: even more debt, but a stable government and a falling deficit.
  5. Limits. The ECB yields are monthly averages, so the October daily closes come from the press. The CDS quotes are from different days (Germany's from July) because there is no free CDS feed, and the 40% recovery rate is a convention, so the implied default risks are rough.
