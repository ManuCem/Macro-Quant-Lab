France vs Italy: what the bond market charges for the risk

10-year bond spreads over Germany (ECB data, monthly, 1999 to Sep 2026) and 5-year credit default swaps, read against the October 2026 protests in both countries.

The short version: for 25 years Italy was the euro area's risky big borrower and France sat close to Germany. That has flipped. France now pays 97 bp over Germany on the monthly average (Sep 2026) and about 154 bp in daily trading, back at its 2011 euro-crisis record. Insuring French debt (CDS) costs more than insuring Italian debt. In 5 of the last 12 months France paid a higher yield than Italy.

3. Credit default swaps: the price of insurance

A CDS is an insurance contract on a government's bonds. The buyer pays a yearly premium and is paid back if the country defaults. The premium is a direct price of default risk, without the noise that moves bond yields (ECB buying, safe-haven demand for Bunds). Rule of thumb: yearly default probability ≈ premium / (1 − recovery), with 40% recovery. So France's 79.5 bp → 0.0080 / 0.60 ≈ 1.33% a year.

France's premium is 10.9x Germany's and above Italy's. It peaked at 81 bp on 2026-10-02, the highest since 2013 (source), but still about a third of its ~250 bp peak in late 2011. A price for insurance is not a forecast that default is likely. There is no free CDS data feed, so these are the latest levels reported in the press, each from a different day:

  • Germany: 7.3 bp on 2026-07-24, Börse München, Bonds Daily
  • Italy: 68.0 bp on 2026-10-09, MacroMicro
  • France: 79.5 bp on 2026-10-15, Investing.com

4. Why France: and why Italy is no longer the worst

France's problem

  • Deficit that will not come down: about 5.4% of GDP in 2026; the 2027 budget only aims for 5.0%. The EU limit is 3%.
  • Debt at a post-war high: about 119% of GDP, and the interest bill rises every time the yield goes up.
  • No stable majority: since the 2024 snap election, governments keep falling (Barnier Dec 2024, Bayrou Sep 2025). Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu leads a minority government that must pass the 2027 budget.
  • Election risk: presidential election in May 2027. Leading candidates promise a lower retirement age (Le Pen) or cancelling the ECB's French debt (Mélenchon). Moody's rates France Aa3 with a negative outlook.

Italy's problem

  • Even more debt: roughly 135% of GDP, the second highest in the euro area after Greece. Italy has always paid a premium for that.
  • But a shrinking deficit and a stable government: Meloni's coalition has governed since Oct 2022, brought the deficit down towards 3% of GDP and won rating upgrades in 2025.
  • Energy shock: higher oil and diesel prices (tension around Iran) hit an energy-importing country hard. The BTP-Bund spread jumped to 126 bp on 2 Oct 2026 and 131 bp on 5 Oct before easing to about 107 bp.
  • The market's verdict: Italy's spread fell from 519 bp in 2011 to under 100 bp, while France's rose. Since late 2025, France has sometimes paid MORE than Italy, which was unthinkable for 25 years.

5. The October 2026 protests

Both countries were hit by cost-of-living unrest on the same weekend (17-18 October). For bond investors the question is not the protests themselves but whether they make it harder to cut the deficit. That is why the French spread reacted more: France has the bigger deficit and no majority to pass its budget.

France

  • From 18 Sep: a teachers' strike in Créteil (Paris suburbs) over staff shortages grows into nationwide student protests over run-down schools.
  • 17 Oct: students, fuel protesters and workers march against the cost of living: more than 550,000 people according to the Interior Ministry. Looting and fires in Paris, Marseille, Lille, Lyon and other cities; Limoges town hall burned. 1,200 arrests, 150 police officers hurt.
  • Markets: the OAT-Bund spread went back above 150 bp (154 bp on 20 Oct) and the 10-year yield approached 5%, the highest since 2002. A first budget vote in the National Assembly is the next test.

Italy

  • From 16 Oct: truckers and farmers block roads in Sicily, Calabria, Basilicata, Puglia and Campania over the price of diesel. Petrol stations run dry and supermarket shelves empty in the South.
  • 16 Oct: "No Meloni Day" student marches in Milan, Turin, Bologna, Rome and other cities.
  • 17-18 Oct: marches against wars and the cost of living turn into riots in Milan, Turin and Bologna. Police report 28 arrests and 141 officers injured. Meloni blames "hooligans"; Transport Minister Salvini meets the hauliers on 20 Oct, with a ministry meeting set for 27 Oct.

6. The yields themselves

The spread is the gap between these lines. All three rose in 2022 when the ECB raised rates. What matters for risk is how far France and Italy sit above Germany.
