How have the two overnight rates moved?

How to read it. EFFR (effective federal funds rate) is the overnight rate banks actually pay each other for unsecured loans, and the rate the Fed steers. SOFR (secured overnight financing rate, from Apr 2018) is the overnight rate on loans backed by Treasuries, the benchmark that replaced LIBOR. 02

What do the terms mean?

Two overnight rates: one on unsecured loans, one on loans backed by Treasuries.

Overnight rate
The interest on a loan repaid the next business day. The shortest, most basic price of money.
EFFR
Effective federal funds rate: the average rate banks pay each other for unsecured overnight loans. The Fed sets a target range and steers EFFR into it.
SOFR
Secured overnight financing rate: the rate on overnight loans backed by US Treasuries (repo). Published by the New York Fed since Apr 2018.
Secured vs unsecured
A secured loan has collateral the lender keeps if it is not repaid; an unsecured one does not, so it can cost a little more.
LIBOR
The old benchmark, based on banks' own estimates of what they would pay. It was replaced by SOFR, which is based on real trades.
Basis point (bp)
0.01 percentage point. A rate going from 3.88% to 3.89% is +1 bp.
