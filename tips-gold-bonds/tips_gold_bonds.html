Real yields · TIPS · Gold

What quietly drives bonds and gold?

The real yield. A bond is a promise of future money. Gold is a metal that promises nothing. Both prices are pulled around by one number: the real yield, the interest you earn after inflation has taken its cut. With the 10-year TIPS at 2.65%, holding $1,000 of gold instead gives up about $26.50 a year of real income.

What is a real yield?

Nominal yield ≈ real yield + expected inflation: 5.1% ≈ 2.65% + 2.3%.

How to read it. The bar is the 10-year nominal yield split into its two parts, drawn to scale: the real yield read from TIPS (orange) and the inflation the market expects, the breakeven (blue). Below it, the four terms the page is built on.

Nominal yield
The interest a normal Treasury pays, in dollars, ignoring inflation.
Real yield
The interest after inflation. Markets read it from TIPS (Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities).
Expected inflation (breakeven)
What the market expects inflation to average. It is the nominal yield minus the TIPS yield.
CPI
Consumer Price Index: the measured rise in prices over the past year. That is actual inflation, not the expectation.

Reference (October 2026): nominal 5.1% vs real 2.65% + breakeven 2.3% = 4.95% (rounding gap 0.15 points). Realised CPI is 3.7%, above the 2.3% the market expects.

02

How does a TIPS keep up with inflation?

$1,000 becomes $1,037.00, plus a $27.48 coupon: 6.45% in dollars, 2.65% after inflation.

How to read it. A TIPS pays a fixed real coupon, but on a principal that is re-scaled by CPI. Read the table top to bottom: one year of a TIPS, first the CPI adjustment, then the coupon paid on the new, larger principal.

If prices rise, the principal rises, and so does the coupon in dollars. That locks in the real return.

When does a TIPS beat a plain bond?

It depends on actual inflation: above 2.39% the TIPS wins, below it the plain bond does.

Why the crossover is not exactly 2.3%: 5.1% − 2.65% = 2.45%, a touch above the quoted 2.3% breakeven. Quoted yields come from different markets and rounding, so a small gap is normal. Real returns here use the exact formula (1+nominal) / (1+inflation) − 1.

04

Why does gold compete with real yields?

At a 2.65% real yield, $1,000 of gold gives up about $26.50 a year versus TIPS.

How to read it. Move the slider to set the 10-year TIPS real yield. The bars compare one year of real income from $1,000 in TIPS and in gold (which pays none); the box below turns the gap into dollars.

Gold pays no coupon and no dividend. Holding it means giving up the real yield you could have earned on a TIPS. The higher that real yield, the more expensive it is to hold gold, so buyers demand a better reason, and the gold price tends to come under pressure. When real yields fall toward zero or below, that cost vanishes and gold becomes easier to hold.

Opportunity cost = the best alternative you gave up. Here: the real yield on a TIPS.

Real yields are the main story, not the only one. The US dollar, central-bank buying and fear in markets also move gold, and the link between gold and real yields has been loose in some years. Treat this as a mental model, not a forecast.

05

What did we learn?

Five takeaways.

  1. Nominal yield ≈ real yield + expected inflation. In October 2026: 5.1% ≈ 2.65% + 2.3%, with a gap of 0.15 points because quoted yields come from different markets and rounding.
  2. A TIPS locks in the real return. CPI re-scales the principal, so $1,000 earned $64.48 in a year at 3.7% CPI: 6.45% in dollars, 2.65% after inflation.
  3. Plain bond or TIPS is a bet on inflation. Both give the same real return at 2.39% actual inflation. If inflation stays at 3.7% CPI, the TIPS wins: the plain bond earns 1.35% real, the TIPS 2.65%.
  4. For gold, the real yield is an opportunity cost. Gold pays no coupon, so at a 2.65% real yield $1,000 of gold gives up about $26.50 a year. When real yields fall toward zero or below, that cost vanishes and gold becomes easier to hold.
  5. Limits. The reference values are typed in by hand for October 2026, not downloaded, and every worked example is illustrative. Real yields are the main story for gold, not the only one: the US dollar, central-bank buying and fear in markets also move it, and the link has been loose in some years. A mental model, not a forecast.
