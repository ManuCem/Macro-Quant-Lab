2. Two stories that pull in opposite directions

Rate fear: yields up, tech down

Most of a tech company's value sits in profits many years away. Those profits are worth less today when interest rates are higher (they are "discounted" at a higher rate). So a day of rising yields is a bad day for the Nasdaq.

Signature: Nasdaq returns and yield changes are negatively correlated.

Rotation: tech down, money into bonds

Investors sell tech and park the money in Treasuries. Buying bonds pushes their price up and their yield down. On good days the reverse happens: bonds are sold to buy tech, and yields rise.

Signature: Nasdaq returns and yield changes are positively correlated (and tech vs bond prices negatively).

Correlation runs from −1 (always opposite) to +1 (always together); near 0 means no linear link. Here it is measured on daily moves: the Nasdaq's % change and the yield's change in basis points (1 bp = 0.01 percentage point).

3. Which story is in charge? Five years of rolling correlation

The correlation between Nasdaq moves and yield changes, measured over the previous 60 trading days (blue) and 20 days (grey), recomputed every day. Above +0.2 = rotation; below −0.2 = rate fear.

The link keeps flipping. Since Oct 2021 the 60-day correlation was positive on 43% of days. Its most negative reading was −0.67 in Jun 2026 (rate fear), its most positive +0.46 in Apr 2025. Today: +0.07 over 60 days, but +0.59 over 20 days: the rotation is recent, a few weeks old. Even 2022, the year of the Fed's fastest hikes, averaged only −0.07 on daily moves, a reminder that "yields up = tech down" is a slow, level story more than a day-to-day one.

7. What the press says

The data matches what was being written in mid-October 2026. A TheStreet column (15 Oct) argued that bonds have become the new safe haven as money rotates out of tech, helped by their high yields. A Bank of America fund-manager survey reported on 13 Oct found that 37% of managers would move money from stocks into bonds if the 10-year yield reached about 6% (it was near 5.2% then), while their overweight in tech was the largest since August 2020: a crowded trade that can unwind into bonds.
