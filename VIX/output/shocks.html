VIX vs realized volatility

Was the VIX right about four market shocks?

Mostly no: it was too low going in. In 4 of 4 shocks the VIX sat below the volatility that followed during the 40 trading days before day 0 (average gap -17.6 to -5.4 points), and on day 0 itself it was too low in 3 of 4. In the 60 days after, it overreacted in 2 and was still too low in 2.

02

What exactly are we measuring?

The VIX on each day, scored against the volatility of the next 21 trading days.

VIX (expected)
The market's expectation of S&P 500 volatility over roughly the next month, read from option prices and quoted as an annualized %. VIX(t) is a claim about the days AFTER t.
Realized volatility, next 21d (actual)
What volatility actually turned out to be: the spread (standard deviation) of the S&P 500's daily log returns over the 21 trading days after each date, annualized (x √252) and in %. It looks at the days after t, not the days before, which would compare the VIX to the wrong time window.
Gap (VIX - realized)
How far the VIX was from the volatility that followed, in annualized vol points. A positive gap means the VIX overpriced volatility; a negative gap means the market underestimated what was coming.
Day 0
The first big panic day of each shock, not necessarily the worst day, so the pre-shock window stays genuinely calm. Each date was checked against the data: worst S&P day or biggest one-day VIX jump of the episode.
Pre-shock and aftermath
Pre-shock: the 40 trading days before day 0. Aftermath: the 60 trading days after it. Counting trading days, not calendar days, keeps weekends and holidays out.
Verdict
Average gap above +2 pts: VIX overreacted. Below -2: VIX underestimated. In between: about right.

07

What did we learn?

Five takeaways.

  1. Going in, the VIX was too low. In 4 of 4 shocks the average gap over the 40 pre-shock days was below -2 points: the market underestimated what was coming.
  2. On day 0 it was still too low in 3 of 4 (exception: Dot-com crash). The biggest miss was -67.4 points in the Covid crash.
  3. Mixed in the aftermath. After day 0 the VIX overreacted in Dot-com crash and 2025 tariff shock and was still too low in 2008 financial crisis and Covid crash.
  4. The shocks were very different. From day 0 to the lowest point the S&P 500 fell between -7.7% and -43.3%, and the bottom came 3 to 622 trading days after day 0.
  5. Limits. Four shocks is a tiny sample, and day 0 was picked by hand. The pre-shock gap is partly built in: from day -21 onward the realized volatility of the next 21 days already includes the shock, which no forecast made on those days could know.
